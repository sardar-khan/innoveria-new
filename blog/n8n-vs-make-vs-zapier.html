n8n vs Make vs Zapier: which automation platform should you actually use?

Published October 24, 2026 · by Ahsan Ilyas, Founder of Innovaria Tech · 10 min read

n8n, Make, and Zapier all promise the same thing: connect your apps so the work happens without you. The catch is that they make very different trade offs on price, power, and how much you have to learn, and picking the wrong one means either a bill that balloons as you grow or a tool that cannot do what you need. This is a plain, vendor neutral comparison. We build on all three every week, so this is what we actually tell clients, not a ranking written to sell one of them.

Short answer: Pick Zapier for the simplest setup and the widest app directory when you have a handful of low volume automations. Pick Make for visual, multi step workflows with branching and data handling at a lower cost than Zapier. Pick n8n for the most power and the lowest cost at scale, if you can self host or have someone technical, because self hosted n8n is not metered per task. In one line: Zapier for easy, Make for value, n8n for power and scale.

Key takeaways

  • Zapier is the easiest to start and has the largest app directory, but its per task pricing climbs steeply as volume grows.
  • Make, formerly Integromat, offers visual multi step workflows with branching and data handling, usually at a lower cost than Zapier.
  • n8n is open source and can be self hosted for near zero cost per run, which makes it by far the cheapest at scale and the most flexible, with room for custom code.
  • The real decision is the pricing model, not the feature list: Zapier and Make meter per task or operation, while self hosted n8n runs on a fixed server cost.
  • Most businesses start on Zapier or Make for speed, then move high volume or complex workflows to n8n once the bill or the limits start to bite.

What each one actually is

Zapier is the original and still the most popular. It connects apps with a simple trigger and action model, when this happens do that, and has the largest directory of ready made connectors, well over 7,000 apps. It is the fastest to learn and the quickest to a first working automation. The trade off is price: Zapier charges per task, so every step of every run counts, and the bill grows with your usage.

Make, formerly Integromat, is a visual builder. Instead of a linear trigger and action, you draw scenarios on a canvas with branching, loops, filters, and data transformation built in. It does more per step than Zapier and prices by operation, which usually works out cheaper at volume. The learning curve is a little steeper, and its app directory, while large, is smaller than Zapier's.

n8n is the open source option. You build workflows from nodes, much like Make, but you can run it on your own server, where it is not metered per task at all: you pay for the hosting, not the runs. It also lets you drop in custom code when a workflow needs logic the pre built nodes do not cover. That makes it the most flexible and, at volume, by far the cheapest. The cost is responsibility: self hosting means someone has to run the server, or you pay for n8n Cloud and give some of the savings back.

The comparison that matters

Features blur together on a sales page. The differences that actually decide the choice are ease, the pricing model, cost at scale, and how far you can push the logic.

Pricing: where the real cost hides

This is the part that surprises people. Zapier and Make both meter usage: Zapier counts tasks, Make counts operations. A single automation that fires often, or a few that run constantly, can turn a small monthly plan into a large one without warning. The tool did not change; your volume did.

Self hosted n8n breaks that link. Because you run it on your own server, a workflow that fires 2,000 times a month and one that fires 200,000 times cost the same to operate: the price of the server. For a business doing tens of thousands of automation runs a month, that difference can be the gap between a few hundred dollars and a few thousand. It is exactly why we self host n8n for high volume clients and reserve Zapier and Make for lower volume or app specific jobs.

Where each one wins

Zapier wins on ease and breadth

If you are new to automation, want to connect two or three popular apps, and your volume is modest, Zapier gets you live the fastest. The huge connector directory means the app you use is almost certainly supported, and you will not touch a line of logic. For a small set of simple automations, the per task price never gets big enough to matter.

Make wins on value and visual workflows

When a workflow needs to branch, loop, or reshape data, Make does it visually and clearly, and its operation pricing usually beats Zapier for the same job. It is the sweet spot for mid volume businesses that have outgrown simple triggers but are not ready to run their own server.

n8n wins on power, cost, and control

For complex logic, high volume, custom code, or strict data control, n8n is in a class of its own. Self hosting keeps the data on infrastructure you own, which matters in regulated fields, and the fixed cost model is unbeatable at scale. The price of admission is a little technical comfort, or a partner who runs it for you.

The honest take: which should you pick?

There is no single winner, only the right fit for your situation. Use this as a quick decision guide:

  • A few simple automations, no technical help: start with Zapier.
  • Visual workflows with branching, watching your budget: use Make.
  • High volume, complex logic, or data you must control: use n8n, self hosted.
  • Not sure, or it has to be reliable: get someone to scope it before you commit to a platform, because switching later is the expensive part.

Plenty of businesses end up using more than one: Zapier for a quick app connection here, n8n carrying the heavy, high volume work there. The platform is a means, not the goal. Frequently asked questions

Is n8n better than Zapier?

It depends on what you value. n8n wins on flexibility and cost: it is open source, can be self hosted for near zero per task fees, and handles complex logic and custom code. Zapier wins on ease and breadth: more ready made connectors and the gentlest learning curve. For simple, low volume automations Zapier is faster to start; for complex or high volume workflows where cost matters, n8n usually comes out ahead.

Is n8n free?

n8n is open source and free to self host on your own server, where you pay only for the hosting, not per task. There is also a paid n8n Cloud plan if you would rather not manage a server. The self hosted route is what makes n8n dramatically cheaper than Zapier or Make at high volume, because runs are not metered per task.

Can Make do everything Zapier does?

For most use cases, yes, and often with more power per dollar. Make, formerly Integromat, uses a visual scenario builder with branching, loops, and data transformation that Zapier charges more to match, and its operation based pricing is usually cheaper at volume. Zapier still has a larger app directory, so the deciding factor is often whether Make connects the specific apps you need.

Which automation tool is best for beginners?

Zapier. Its trigger and action model is the simplest to grasp, the app directory is the largest, and you can build a useful automation in minutes without touching logic or code. Make is the next step up when you need branching or data handling, and n8n is best once you are comfortable with how data flows between steps.

Do I need to know how to code to use n8n?

No. n8n is a visual, node based builder you can use without writing code for the large majority of workflows. The difference is that when you do need custom logic, n8n lets you drop in a code step, which Zapier and Make limit or charge more for. So it rewards technical users without requiring them.

Which is cheapest at scale?

Self hosted n8n, by a wide margin. Because Zapier and Make meter by task or operation, costs climb as volume grows. Self hosted n8n runs on a fixed server cost no matter how many times a workflow fires, so a business running tens of thousands of automation runs a month often pays a fraction of the equivalent Zapier bill.
