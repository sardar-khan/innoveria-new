What AI consulting actually includes before you build anything

Published August 14, 2026 · by Ahsan Ilyas, Founder of Innovaria Tech · 9 min read

Search around and you will find AI readiness assessments priced from two thousand to eight thousand dollars, before a single workflow gets built. That is a real, common price in this market right now. It is also a strange one, since the entire point of an assessment is to find out whether automation is worth doing in your business at all. Paying thousands of dollars just to get an answer that might be no is a rough deal, and it is exactly why this stage deserves a closer look before you sign anything.

Quick answer: Real AI consulting maps your actual workflows, identifies where automation would genuinely save time or money, and tells you honestly when it would not. It should end with a specific, scoped recommendation, not a vague strategy deck. The assessment itself should be low cost or free, since a consultant who needs to charge thousands just to look at your business before recommending anything has the incentives backwards. The paid work should start once there is a real, agreed plan to build.

Key takeaways

  • A real consulting engagement produces a specific, scoped recommendation, not a generic strategy document that could apply to any business.
  • The assessment stage should cost little or nothing. The paid work belongs to the actual build, not to being told what to build.
  • A good consultant will sometimes tell you automation is not worth it yet, and that answer is more valuable than a forced yes.
  • Watch for consultants who lead with tool names before understanding your workflow, and for pricing that is vague until after you have committed time to a sales process.
  • Full builds after a consultation typically cost between one thousand and five thousand dollars, a fraction of the multi tier enterprise consulting pricing common elsewhere in this market.

Why this stage gets skipped or done badly

AI consulting exists because most small businesses know they should probably be using AI for something, and have no reliable way to figure out what that something actually is. That uncertainty is exactly what a rushed or poorly incentivized consultant takes advantage of.

A few patterns show up constantly in how this stage goes wrong.

Assessments get priced like the main product. When the readiness assessment itself costs several thousand dollars, the incentive shifts toward finding something to recommend, since a report that says do nothing yet does not justify the invoice.

Recommendations arrive generic instead of specific. A strategy document listing marketing automation, customer service AI, and data analytics as opportunities is not a plan. It is a summary of what AI can do in general, dressed up as advice for your business specifically.

Tool names show up before the problem does. A consultant who opens with which platform to buy, rather than which workflow is actually broken, is selling a product relationship, not solving a problem.

Nobody says no. Every engagement ends with a recommendation to build something, because that is how the consultant gets paid next, even when the honest answer is that the business is not ready yet or the return does not justify the cost.

What a real engagement should actually include

Strip away the slide deck language and a genuinely useful consulting engagement covers a short, specific list of things.

A real look at your actual workflows. Not a questionnaire, an actual walk through of how work currently moves through your business, where the delays are, and where a person is doing something a system could do just as well.

A specific, scoped recommendation. Not marketing automation as a category, but the exact workflow worth automating first, what it would connect to, and roughly what it would take to build.

An honest cost and return estimate. A real number, or a real range, tied to your actual volume and your actual team size, not an industry average pulled from a report.

A clear answer when automation is not the right move yet. If your documentation is not organized enough, or your volume is too low to justify the build, a real consultant tells you that directly instead of finding something smaller to sell you anyway.

A plan that does not require the same consultant to execute it. A good recommendation should be specific enough that another competent team could build it, even if you choose to build it with us.

Red flags worth watching for

A few signs tend to show up together when a consulting engagement is built to sell rather than to actually help.

Vague pricing until you have already invested time. A real consultant can give you a rough range early. One who insists on a full discovery call before naming any number at all is often optimizing for a sales process, not a straight answer.

A recommendation that always matches whatever the consultant happens to sell. If every engagement somehow concludes that their specific platform or service is the answer, the assessment was not neutral to begin with.

No willingness to say the honest answer is not yet. This is the single clearest signal. A business that is too small, too undocumented, or too low volume to benefit yet deserves to hear that, not a scaled down sale.

Case studies with numbers nobody can verify. Impressive statistics with no named client, no link, and no way to check them are worth treating with real skepticism, regardless of how confident the framing sounds.

What a realistic engagement looks like

Picture a small business owner who knows they are spending too much time on repetitive work but has no clear sense of where to start. A real first conversation walks through the actual day to day, what comes in, what has to happen next, and where a person is currently doing something that follows a predictable pattern. That conversation usually surfaces one or two clear candidates, not ten vague possibilities, along with a rough cost and a realistic timeline.

From there, the honest outcome is one of three things: a specific build worth doing now, a smaller first step worth testing before committing to more, or a direct answer that now is not the right time. All three are a legitimate result of a consulting engagement done well. Only one of them keeps the consultant's calendar full, which is exactly why the incentive structure of the assessment stage matters as much as the advice itself.

Frequently asked questions

Why is your assessment free when others charge thousands?

Because charging for the assessment creates pressure to recommend something regardless of whether it is the right call. We would rather earn the build by giving you an honest, specific answer first, even when that answer is not yet.

What if the answer is that automation is not worth it for us right now?

We tell you that directly, along with what would need to change, more volume, more organized documentation, a different priority, for it to make sense later. That is a legitimate, useful outcome, not a failed sales call.

How long does a consulting conversation take?

A first conversation covering your actual workflows usually takes under an hour. You get a specific recommendation and a rough cost range within a few days, not weeks of back and forth.

Do you specialize in one industry or one type of automation?

No. We work across industries including manufacturing, logistics, and education, and the consultation is what determines which service actually fits your business, rather than us starting with a product and looking for a use for it.
