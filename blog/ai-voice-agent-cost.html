How much does an AI voice agent cost in 2026? The real numbers for a small business

Published October 23, 2026 · by Ahsan Ilyas, Founder of Innovaria Tech · 11 min read

Quick answer: An AI voice agent has two costs. The first is the build: a custom agent connected to your calendar, CRM and transfer rules typically costs $1,000 to $5,000 once. The second is running it: you pay per minute of conversation, usually around $0.06 to $0.15 a minute for the full stack, plus a few small monthly fees. For a clinic taking 400 calls a month, that comes to roughly $155 to $235 a month. A full time receptionist costs about $3,170 a month in wages alone.

Key takeaways

  • The "per minute" price on most pricing pages covers only part of the bill. The AI model, the voice and the phone line often charge separately.
  • Build cost depends on what the agent connects to, not on how clever it sounds. Booking into a real calendar and transferring calls with a summary take most of the setup work.
  • Small businesses usually spend more on the build than on minutes in the first year. After that, the monthly bill stays low unless call volume jumps.
  • Compare an AI agent with the calls you miss today, not with a perfect receptionist. Most small teams compare it with voicemail.
  • Keep a person in the loop. The cheapest setup that frustrates callers costs more than a slightly pricier one that hands off well.

What you actually pay for

An AI voice agent bill has two parts: a one time build and a monthly running cost. Vendors tend to talk about only one of them, depending on what they sell.

The build covers the work that makes the agent useful for your business:

  • Writing the call flows, greeting and answers in your voice
  • Connecting the agent to your booking system so it books real slots
  • Setting transfer rules, so complex or upset callers reach a person with a summary
  • Logging every call, transcript and outcome in your CRM
  • Testing against your hardest real call scenarios before launch

The running cost covers the minutes the agent spends on the phone and the tools underneath it. A self serve platform skips most of the build, but you still do that work yourself, or you live without it.

Three things push the per minute number up:

  1. Premium voices. A more natural voice can cost more than six times as much as a standard one. Test the standard voice first; callers often cannot tell.
  2. Bigger AI models. A top tier model can cost three to eight times more a minute than a small one. Most receptionist work (booking, hours, directions, intake questions) runs well on a smaller model.
  3. Add ons. Knowledge base lookups, noise filtering, removing personal data from transcripts and quality checks each add a little per minute.

Hidden costs that pricing pages leave out

Most surprises on an AI voice agent bill come from five places:

  • Rounding. Some platforms round every call up to a full minute. A 20 second wrong number then costs as much as a 60 second call. Telnyx notes this on its own pricing page.
  • Transfers. When the agent hands a call to a person, the AI fee stops, but you keep paying for the phone line until the call ends. Retell's pricing FAQ says this directly.
  • Concurrent calls. Platforms often include a set number of calls at once and charge for more. Retell includes 20 and then charges $8 a month for each extra line. A small clinic rarely needs more, but a busy dispatch desk might.
  • Silence and hold time. Billing continues while a caller looks for their insurance card. Long holds add up.
  • Your own time. A self serve tool costs less per month but needs someone to write prompts, test edge cases and fix things when a booking fails. Our step by step guide on how to set up an AI voice agent shows exactly what that work involves.

Self serve platform or custom build: which one fits

Pick by how much the phone matters to your revenue, not by the sticker price.

Choose a self serve platform if:

  • You mostly need after hours message taking
  • Your booking happens on a simple online calendar
  • Someone on your team enjoys testing and tweaking
  • Your budget is under $1,000 to start

Choose a custom build if:

  • The agent must book into your practice software or CRM
  • Calls need routing by service, location or urgency
  • You work in healthcare, legal or finance, where handoff rules and data handling matter
  • You want every call logged with a transcript and a follow up task, without anyone copying details by hand

A custom build typically costs $1,000 to $5,000 once, plus $50 to $300 a month to run, depending on call volume. You own every account it runs on. The home services answering guide walks through the same decision for contractors.

Is an AI voice agent worth it? The payback math

Multiply three numbers to see what missed calls cost you each month:

Missed calls per month × share that would have booked × average value of a booking

A made up example, not client figures: a clinic misses 60 calls a month. If one in four would have booked and the average visit is worth $150, the clinic leaves $2,250 on the table each month. A voice agent that recovers even a third of those bookings brings in about $750 a month. That covers the running cost several times over and pays back a $3,000 build in about six months.

Two numbers decide whether it pays for you: how many calls you miss, and what one booking is worth. Check your phone system's missed call report for one week before you buy anything.

Compliance costs to plan for

Rules do not show up on a pricing page, but they shape what you build:

  • Outbound calls need consent. In February 2024 the FCC confirmed that AI generated voices count as "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so outbound AI calls need the caller's prior consent (Cooley's summary of the ruling). The ruling targets outbound calls, not inbound answering, but AI reminder and follow up calls can fall under it. Collect consent before you switch them on.
  • Recording consent varies by state. Some US states require every party on a call to agree to recording. Put a short disclosure in the greeting.
  • Healthcare needs HIPAA appropriate platforms. Choose vendors that sign a business associate agreement, and keep clinical questions with your staff. Our post on voice agents for clinics covers where to draw that line.

These rules rarely add much to the monthly bill. They add time to the build, which is one more reason the build costs more than the minutes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an AI voice agent cost per month?

Most small businesses pay $50 to $300 a month to run an AI voice agent, depending on call volume. The bill is mostly call minutes at roughly $0.06 to $0.15 each, plus a phone number and workflow tools. A clinic with 400 three minute calls a month pays about $155 to $235.

How much does it cost to set up an AI voice agent?

A custom build connected to your calendar, CRM and transfer rules typically costs $1,000 to $5,000 once. A self serve platform can cost little or nothing to start, but you do the setup, testing and maintenance yourself.

Is an AI voice agent cheaper than a receptionist?

Yes, by a wide margin on cost alone. A full time receptionist earns a median of $38,010 a year in the US, about $3,170 a month before taxes and benefits. An AI agent costs a fraction of that, but it handles routine calls only. Most businesses use it to cover evenings, weekends and overflow, not to replace staff.

What is the cost per minute of an AI voice agent?

Expect about $0.06 to $0.15 a minute for the full stack: voice engine, voice, AI model and phone line. Premium voices and larger AI models can push it past $0.20. Always check whether a quoted rate includes all four layers.

Are there hidden fees with AI phone agents?

Watch for calls rounded up to a full minute, phone line charges that continue after a transfer, fees for extra concurrent calls, and billing during hold or silence. Ask any vendor to show a sample bill at your call volume.

How long does it take to build an AI voice agent?

One to two weeks for a straightforward answering and booking agent, and two to four weeks for a full system with transfers, CRM logging and missed call text back.

Do I own the AI voice agent you build?

Yes. Phone numbers, recordings, transcripts and workflows run in accounts registered to your business. If you stop working with us, the agent keeps answering.
