The drag we see in every shop

Where orders quietly go to competitors

RFQs aging in a shared inbox, afternoons lost to order status calls, and supplier follow ups that slip. All three are automatable without touching the shop floor.

RFQs sit while competitors quote

The request lands in sales@ at 7 am, gets opened after lunch, and reaches an estimator tomorrow. The buyer sent the same drawing to four shops, and the first serious reply usually frames the deal.

Afternoons lost to where is my order

Customers call because they cannot see what your ERP already knows. The front office looks up the same job status, relays it, and hangs up in time for the next identical call.

Supplier follow ups slip and stall production

A purchase order goes out and nobody confirms it. Two weeks later the material has not shipped, the production schedule slides, and your customer hears about it after the promise date.

The system we build for you

Questions shops ask

Manufacturing automation, answered directly

What are AI agents for manufacturing?

Software assistants that handle the front office work around your shop: capturing RFQs and routing specs to the right estimator within minutes, following up on quotes you sent, answering order status calls and emails from live ERP data, and chasing suppliers for confirmations and delivery dates. Your estimators and schedulers keep the decisions; the agents do the relaying and the reminding.

Can it connect to our ERP?

If your ERP has an API or can produce exports, yes. We read order status, delivery dates, and production stages from the system you already run and never ask you to replace it. Plenty of shops we talk to run part of the operation on spreadsheets, and we work from those too.

How fast can RFQ response get?

The buyer gets an acknowledgment in under 60 seconds, any hour, confirming what you received and what happens next. The parsed specs land with the right estimator within minutes instead of sitting in a shared inbox until someone checks it. How fast the full quote goes out still depends on your estimating, but the race no longer starts hours late.

What does manufacturing automation cost?

A complete system typically lands between $1,000 and $5,000 to build plus $50 to $300 in monthly platform fees. One order that would have gone to the faster quote usually covers it. You get a fixed quote after a free audit of your RFQ and order flow, and there is no hourly billing.

Will it work for a small shop without an ERP?

Yes. If your orders live in spreadsheets, QuickBooks, or a whiteboard photo someone emails around, we start there. RFQ intake, quote follow up, and supplier chasing need a reliable inbox and a place to record status, not a six figure ERP. Many shops use the automation build as the nudge to tidy that up.
